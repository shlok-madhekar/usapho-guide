## Two conservation laws, one strategy

Energy and momentum answer different questions. Momentum $\vec p = m\vec v$
is conserved whenever external forces vanish (or act too briefly to matter).
Mechanical energy is conserved only when non-conservative forces do no work.

$$W = \int \vec F \cdot d\vec x, \qquad K = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2, \qquad \vec p_i = \vec p_f$$

## Collisions decoded

- Elastic: momentum and kinetic energy both conserved
- Perfectly inelastic: objects stick; maximum KE is lost (but never all of it, if the system moves)
- 1D elastic trick: relative velocity reverses, $v_{1i} - v_{2i} = -(v_{1f} - v_{2f})$
- Equal masses colliding elastically in 2D separate at 90°

<Problem
  id="np-em-1"
  number={1}
  title="Ballistic block"
  difficulty="Normal"
  answer={2}
  unit="m/s"
>
  A 10 g bullet moving at 400 m/s embeds itself in a stationary 1.99 kg block
  on a frictionless surface. What is the speed of the combined object
  afterward, in m/s?

  <Hint>Perfectly inelastic collision: only momentum survives.</Hint>
  <Solution>
    $$v = \frac{m_b v_b}{m_b + M} = \frac{(0.01)(400)}{2.00} = 2 \text{ m/s}$$

    Kinetic energy drops from 800 J to 4 J. That missing 796 J became heat and
    deformation, which is why you cannot use energy conservation across the
    impact.
  </Solution>
</Problem>

<Problem
  id="np-em-2"
  number={2}
  title="Loop the loop"
  difficulty="Hard"
  answer={5}
  unit="m"
>
  A cart starts from rest at height $h$ and slides without friction into a
  vertical circular loop of radius 2 m. Using $g = 10$ m/s², what is the
  minimum $h$, in meters, so the cart maintains contact at the top of the
  loop?

  <Hint>
    At the minimum speed, gravity alone supplies the centripetal force at the
    top: $mg = mv^2/r$.
  </Hint>
  <Solution>
    The critical condition at the top gives $v^2 = gr$. Energy conservation
    from the start to the top (height $2r$):

    $$mgh = mg(2r) + \tfrac12 m v^2 = 2mgr + \tfrac12 mgr \implies h = \tfrac52 r = 5 \text{ m}$$
  </Solution>
</Problem>
