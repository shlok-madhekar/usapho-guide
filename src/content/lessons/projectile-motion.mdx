## Two problems, one clock

Nothing pushes a projectile sideways, so its horizontal velocity never
changes. Gravity pulls it down, so its vertical velocity changes at a constant
rate. Those are two separate one-dimensional problems. The only thing they
share is the clock.

$$x(t) = v_0\cos\theta\; t, \qquad y(t) = v_0\sin\theta\; t - \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2$$

Solve the first for $t$ and substitute into the second and the time drops out,
leaving the path itself:

$$y = x\tan\theta - \frac{g\,x^2}{2v_0^2\cos^2\theta}$$

A parabola, fixed entirely by the launch speed and angle.

## Results worth memorizing

For launch and landing at the same height:

$$R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}, \qquad H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}, \qquad T = \frac{2 v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$$

Two consequences are tested constantly. Range peaks at 45°, and because
$\sin 2\theta$ is symmetric about that peak, any pair of complementary angles
covers the same ground: 30° and 60° land in the same place, though the steeper
one takes longer to get there.

On a slope of angle $\phi$, the range is largest at
$\theta = 45^\circ + \phi/2$, measured from the horizontal. Uphill you aim
higher; downhill you aim flatter.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  Every formula above assumes the projectile lands at its launch height. A
  cliff, a slope, or a bounce breaks that assumption, and the range formula
  quietly gives the wrong answer instead of failing loudly. When the ground
  moves, go back to the two component equations.
</Aside>

## Working with the components

When the geometry stops being symmetric, the reliable move is to write down
$x(t)$ and $y(t)$, impose whatever condition the problem gives you (lands on a
slope, clears a wall, hits a moving target), and solve. It is slower than
recalling a formula and it never fails.

A worked instance: a ball thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from a 45 m cliff. The
vertical drop sets the clock, $t = \sqrt{2h/g} = 3$ s, and the horizontal
motion is uniform, so it lands 45 m out. At no point did the two directions
talk to each other.
