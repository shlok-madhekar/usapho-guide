## A conductor is one number

Charges in a conductor move until nothing pushes them, so in equilibrium
$\vec E = 0$ inside the metal, excess charge sits on surfaces, and the whole
body — surface, interior, and any disconnected face — is at a single
potential. The last statement is the useful one, because it is a condition on
$V$, and $V$ is a scalar you can set equal between two places and solve.

Just outside the surface the field is perpendicular and

$$E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$$

twice the infinite-sheet value, because there is no field on the metal side to
share the flux with.

## Induced charge is bookkeeping with two constraints

Bring $+q$ near a neutral conducting slab. Electrons pile up on the near face
and leave the far face positive. The two constraints that fix the amounts are
always the same: total charge is conserved on each isolated conductor, and the
field inside the metal must vanish.

Run them on a shell with inner radius $a$, outer radius $b$, carrying net
charge $Q$, with $+q$ at the center. A Gaussian sphere inside the metal must
enclose zero, so the inner surface holds $-q$. Conservation then puts $Q+q$ on
the outer surface. If the shell is grounded, the second constraint changes:
charge flows in from the earth to hold $V=0$, the outer surface ends up bare,
and the exterior field disappears.

## Capacitance

Put $+Q$ on one conductor and $-Q$ on a nearby one. The potential difference
is proportional to $Q$, and the ratio depends only on geometry:

$$C = \frac{Q}{V}$$

For parallel plates of area $A$ and gap $d$, the field between them is
$\sigma/\varepsilon_0 = Q/\varepsilon_0 A$, uniform, so $V = Ed$ and

$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$$

Wider plates hold more charge at the same voltage; a bigger gap means a longer
climb against the same field, so less.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  "Grounded" does not mean "uncharged" — it means held at $V = 0$ with charge
  free to arrive. And $C$ is fixed by geometry: it does not change when you
  charge the plates. If $C$ appears to depend on $Q$ in your algebra, a
  variable is being held constant that should not be.
</Aside>
