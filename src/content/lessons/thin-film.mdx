## Two reflections, one film

Light striking a soap film or an oil slick reflects twice: once off the top
surface, once off the bottom. Those two beams recombine and interfere, and
since white light contains every wavelength, some colors cancel and others
survive. That is where the color comes from.

At normal incidence the second beam travels an extra $2t$ through the film. But
the wavelength inside the film is shortened to $\lambda/n$, so the phase cost
of that path is

$$\Delta\phi_{\text{path}} = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,2nt$$

The quantity $2nt$ is the optical path difference. Using the vacuum wavelength
with the geometric thickness and forgetting $n$ is the standard slip.

## Counting the flips

Reflection off a medium of higher index inverts the wave — a half-wavelength
phase shift, exactly like a pulse on a string reflecting off a fixed end.
Reflection off a lower index does not.

So the procedure is mechanical. Write down the three indices in order. Check
each of the two reflections and count how many flips occur.

Two flips, or zero, cancel each other and the film brightens when
$2nt = m\lambda$. Exactly one flip adds a net half cycle and the condition
inverts: brightness at $2nt = (m + \tfrac{1}{2})\lambda$.

Soap in air is air-soap-air: the top reflection flips, the bottom does not.
One flip. An anti-reflective coating on glass is air-coating-glass with
$1 < n_c < n_g$, so both reflections flip — two flips, and a quarter-wave
thickness kills the reflection.

## The black film

Let a vertical soap film drain until the top is far thinner than a wavelength.
Then $2nt \to 0$, the path contributes no phase, and the single flip is all
that remains. The two beams are a half cycle apart for every wavelength at
once, so the whole visible spectrum cancels and the top of the film looks
black — just before it bursts.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  The flip count depends on the medium on both sides, not just the film. An oil
  film on water and the same film on glass have opposite conditions, because
  water has a lower index than the oil and glass a higher one. Never carry a
  memorized half-integer rule between problems; recount the flips each time.
</Aside>
