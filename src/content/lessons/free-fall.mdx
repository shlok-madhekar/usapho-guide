## Constant gravity

Near the surface of the Earth, gravity pulls every object down with the same
acceleration $g \approx 9.8$ m/s², regardless of mass. Take up as positive and
the whole problem is one equation and its integrals:

$$a = -g, \qquad v = v_0 - gt, \qquad y = y_0 + v_0 t - \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2$$

Two facts save time. The velocity is zero at the top, which fixes the rise time
at $t = v_0/g$. And the motion is symmetric about that instant, so an object
returns to any height with the same speed it had going up, just reversed.

For a drop from rest through height $h$, energy or kinematics both give
$v = \sqrt{2gh}$ and $t = \sqrt{2h/g}$. Doubling the height does not double the
landing speed; it multiplies it by $\sqrt{2}$.

## The moving platform

The single most common error in free-fall problems is assuming a released
object starts at rest. It starts with whatever velocity the platform had.

A ball dropped from a balloon rising at 5 m/s leaves with $v_0 = +5$ m/s. It
keeps climbing for half a second before it starts falling, and it lands later
and faster than a ball dropped from a stationary balloon at the same height.
The word "dropped" describes the hand, not the physics.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  Read the release condition, not the verb. Ask what the object's velocity was
  the instant before it was let go, and use that as $v_0$ with its sign. A
  descending elevator gives a negative $v_0$, a rising one gives a positive
  $v_0$, and only a genuinely stationary support gives zero.
</Aside>

## When drag matters

Real falls are not constant-acceleration for long. Air resistance grows with
speed, and at low speeds it is roughly linear:

$$m\frac{dv}{dt} = mg - bv$$

The acceleration starts at $g$ and shrinks as $v$ grows. It reaches zero when
the drag balances the weight, at the terminal speed $v_t = mg/b$. The object
does not overshoot and come back; it approaches $v_t$ from below, exponentially,
with time constant $m/b$.

For fast objects the drag goes as $v^2$ instead, giving $v_t = \sqrt{mg/c}$.
The exponential shape changes, but the logic does not: set the net force to
zero and solve for the speed at which nothing further changes.
