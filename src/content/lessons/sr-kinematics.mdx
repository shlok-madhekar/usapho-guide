## Relativity

Two postulates: the laws of physics are the same in every inertial frame, and
light travels at $c$ for every observer. Everything strange follows from
insisting on both at once.

$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}, \qquad \Delta t = \gamma\Delta t_0, \qquad L = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}$$

Moving clocks run slow and moving objects contract along the direction of
motion. The subscript zero always marks the frame where the measurement is
simplest: proper time is measured where the events happen at one place, proper
length where the object is at rest.

Energy and momentum get corrected too:

$$E = \gamma mc^2, \qquad p = \gamma mv, \qquad E^2 = (pc)^2 + (mc^2)^2$$

That last relation is the useful one, because it holds for massless particles
as well, giving $E = pc$ for a photon.

<Aside label="Worth remembering">
  Muons created high in the atmosphere reach the ground even though their
  lifetime should not permit it. In our frame their clocks run slow; in their
  frame the atmosphere is contracted. Both descriptions give the same answer,
  which is the point.
</Aside>
