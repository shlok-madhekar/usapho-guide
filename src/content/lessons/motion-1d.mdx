## Definitions, then everything else

Velocity is how position changes, acceleration is how velocity changes:

$$v = \frac{dx}{dt}, \qquad a = \frac{dv}{dt}$$

Hold $a$ constant and integrate twice and you get the three equations you will
use for the rest of the year:

$$v = v_0 + at, \qquad x = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2, \qquad v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\,\Delta x$$

The third is the useful one, because it contains no time. Whenever a problem
gives you speeds and a distance but never mentions seconds, that is the
equation it wants.

## Reading graphs

Most exam questions on this topic are graph questions in disguise.

- The slope of a position graph is velocity; the slope of a velocity graph is acceleration.
- The area under a velocity graph is displacement. It is signed area, so time spent moving backwards subtracts.
- The area under an acceleration graph is the change in velocity, not the velocity itself.

## Free fall

Near the ground, and ignoring air, everything accelerates downward at the same
$g$ regardless of mass. The subtlety is almost never the acceleration; it is
the initial condition. An object released from a moving platform leaves with
the platform's velocity, which is why a sandbag dropped from a rising balloon
first goes up.
