## The core idea

Projectile motion is two **independent** 1D problems wearing a trench coat.
Horizontally, nothing pushes: constant velocity. Vertically, gravity acts:
constant acceleration. The clock $t$ is the only thing the two directions share.

$$x(t) = v_0\cos\theta\, t, \qquad y(t) = v_0\sin\theta\, t - \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2$$

Eliminating $t$ gives the trajectory, a parabola whose shape depends only on
the launch conditions:

$$y = x\tan\theta - \frac{g x^2}{2v_0^2\cos^2\theta}$$

## Key results worth knowing cold

- Range on flat ground: $R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$
- Max height: $H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$
- Flight time: $T = \frac{2 v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$
- On an incline of angle $\phi$, maximize range by launching at
  $\theta = 45^\circ + \phi/2$ from horizontal

<Callout label="Olympiad trap" warn>
  These formulas assume launch and landing at the same height. The moment a
  cliff, incline, or bounce appears, go back to the component equations.
  Plugging into $R = v_0^2\sin2\theta/g$ blindly is the classic wrong answer
  choice.
</Callout>

## Solve it here

<Problem
  id="np-proj-1"
  number={1}
  title="Off the cliff"
  difficulty="Normal"
  answer={45}
  unit="m"
>
  A ball is thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from a cliff 45 m high. Using
  $g = 10$ m/s², how far from the base of the cliff does it land, in meters?

  <Hint>
    The vertical motion alone sets the flight time. Find it from
    $h = \tfrac12 g t^2$, then use it horizontally.
  </Hint>
  <Solution>
    Time to fall 45 m:

    $$t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2(45)}{10}} = 3 \text{ s}$$

    Horizontal range: $x = v_x t = 15 \times 3 = 45$ m. The horizontal and
    vertical motions never mix.
  </Solution>
</Problem>

<Problem
  id="np-proj-2"
  number={2}
  title="Same range, two angles"
  difficulty="Hard"
  answer={65}
  unit="°"
  tolerancePct={1}
>
  A projectile launched at 25° travels a certain range on flat ground. What
  other launch angle, in degrees, gives the same range at the same speed?

  <Hint>
    Range goes as $\sin 2\theta$, and $\sin(180^\circ - x) = \sin x$.
  </Hint>
  <Solution>
    The range formula $R = v_0^2 \sin 2\theta / g$ gives equal ranges when
    $2\theta_1$ and $2\theta_2$ are supplementary:

    $$2\theta_2 = 180^\circ - 50^\circ \implies \theta_2 = 65^\circ$$

    In general, complementary launch angles $\theta$ and $90^\circ - \theta$
    share a range.
  </Solution>
</Problem>
