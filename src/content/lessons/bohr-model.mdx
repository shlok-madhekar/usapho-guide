## One extra rule

Classically, an electron orbiting a proton obeys Coulomb's law with the force
supplying the centripetal acceleration, and any radius is allowed. Bohr added a
single restriction: angular momentum comes in whole multiples of $\hbar$.

$$L = mvr = n\hbar, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$$

Combine that with $\dfrac{ke^2}{r^2} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ and both $r$ and $v$
are forced to discrete values. The radii grow as $n^2$:

$$r_n = n^2 a_0, \qquad a_0 = 0.529\ \text{Å}$$

The total energy is kinetic plus potential, and for any inverse-square orbit
$K = -U/2$, so $E = U/2 = -ke^2/2r$. Substituting $r_n$ gives the ladder:

$$E_n = -\frac{13.6\ \text{eV}}{n^2}$$

Negative means bound. The ground state at $n = 1$ sits 13.6 eV below the free
electron, which is exactly the ionisation energy of hydrogen — the model's
first real prediction.

## Lines are differences

Levels are not what a spectrometer sees. It sees photons, and a photon is
emitted when the electron drops from one level to another, carrying the gap:

$$hf = E_{n_i} - E_{n_f} = 13.6\ \text{eV}\left(\frac{1}{n_f^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2}\right)$$

Every transition ending at $n_f = 2$ lands in the visible — that is the Balmer
series, the four coloured lines in a hydrogen tube. Transitions to $n_f = 1$
are ultraviolet, to $n_f = 3$ infrared.

Absorption is the same arithmetic run backwards. A photon is swallowed only if
its energy matches a gap exactly, so white light passed through cold hydrogen
comes out with dark lines in precisely the positions where hot hydrogen glows.
The pattern identifies the element regardless of which direction you look.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  The energy of an emitted photon is a *difference* between levels, never the
  level itself. A drop from $n = 3$ to $n = 2$ gives 1.89 eV, not 1.51 eV.
  Compute both energies, subtract, then convert.
</Aside>

## What it cannot do

The model works for hydrogen and for any one-electron ion, where replacing
$e^2$ with $Ze^2$ scales the ladder by $Z^2$. It fails for helium onward,
because electrons repel each other and the neat orbit picture dissolves.

It also says nothing about line intensities, and it offers no reason why the
orbits should be stable — an accelerating charge ought to radiate. Quantum
mechanics later supplies both, and reproduces $E_n$ exactly while discarding
the orbits entirely. Keep the energy ladder; treat the circular paths as
scaffolding.
