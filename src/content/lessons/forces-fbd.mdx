## The FBD ritual

Every dynamics problem is the same four steps: isolate one body, draw every
force acting **on it** (never forces it exerts), choose axes (usually along
the acceleration), and write $\sum F = ma$ per axis.

$$\sum F_x = ma_x, \qquad \sum F_y = ma_y$$

## The usual suspects

- Weight $mg$, always down
- Normal force $N$, perpendicular to the surface, and *not* always $mg$
- Tension: same magnitude throughout an ideal (massless) rope
- Friction: $f_s \le \mu_s N$ (an inequality!), $f_k = \mu_k N$

<Callout label="Olympiad trap" warn>
  Static friction is whatever value keeps the object from sliding, up to its
  maximum. Writing $f = \mu_s N$ before verifying the object is on the verge
  of slipping is the most common F=ma error in this topic.
</Callout>

  title="Blocks on a string"
  difficulty="Normal"
  answer={2}
  unit="m/s²"
>
  A 3 kg block and a 2 kg block hang from a frictionless pulley (Atwood
  machine). Using $g = 10$ m/s², what is the magnitude of their acceleration,
  in m/s²?

  <Hint>
    Write Newton's second law for each mass separately, with the same tension
    and the same acceleration magnitude.
  </Hint>
  <Solution>
    Adding the two equations eliminates the tension:

    $$a = \frac{(m_1 - m_2)g}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{(3-2)(10)}{5} = 2 \text{ m/s}^2$$
  </Solution>
</Problem>
