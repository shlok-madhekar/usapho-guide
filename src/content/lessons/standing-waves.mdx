## Adding two waves

Superposition says the displacement at a point is the sum of what each wave
would have produced alone. Send two identical waves in opposite directions
along a string and add them:

$$A\sin(kx - \omega t) + A\sin(kx + \omega t) = 2A\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)$$

The $x$ and $t$ dependence has separated. Nothing travels any more. Every
point oscillates at the same frequency $\omega$, and its amplitude $2A\sin(kx)$
depends only on where it sits. Points where $\sin(kx) = 0$ never move — nodes,
spaced half a wavelength apart. Halfway between them are antinodes swinging
with amplitude $2A$.

That factorization is the whole content of "standing wave." It is worth
deriving once rather than remembering as a picture.

## Modes of a string

A string clamped at both ends forces a node at $x = 0$ and $x = L$. Only
wavelengths satisfying $L = n\lambda/2$ survive, giving the harmonic series
$f_n = nv/2L$ with $v = \sqrt{T/\mu}$.

A real pluck is not a single mode. It is a superposition of many, with
amplitudes fixed by the initial shape. Pluck at the midpoint and every even
harmonic has a node there, so those modes get zero amplitude — the timbre
changes with plucking position, which is why guitarists pick near the bridge
for a brighter sound.

## Why a closed pipe skips harmonics

In a sound wave the boundary condition is about displacement of the air. A
closed end blocks motion, so it must be a displacement node. An open end is
free to move and sits at an antinode.

A node at one end and an antinode at the other means the pipe holds a quarter
wavelength, or three quarters, or five — never a half. So

$$L = \frac{(2n-1)\lambda}{4}, \qquad f_n = \frac{(2n-1)v}{4L}$$

Only odd multiples of the fundamental appear, and the fundamental is half that
of an open pipe of equal length.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  Pressure and displacement are out of phase by a quarter wavelength. A
  displacement node is a pressure antinode. If a problem gives you a pressure
  graph and you read its zeros as fixed ends, every mode number comes out
  wrong. Decide which quantity the diagram shows before counting.
</Aside>
