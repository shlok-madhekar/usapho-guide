## Describing motion

Everything in kinematics flows from two definitions: $v = \frac{dx}{dt}$ and
$a = \frac{dv}{dt}$. For constant acceleration these integrate into the
familiar suvat equations:

$$v = v_0 + at, \qquad x = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2, \qquad v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x$$

The third equation is the time-free one. Reach for it whenever a problem never
mentions time.

## Graphs are the real language

- Slope of $x$&ndash;$t$ is velocity; slope of $v$&ndash;$t$ is acceleration
- Area under $v$&ndash;$t$ is displacement (signed area, not distance)
- Area under $a$&ndash;$t$ is the change in velocity

<Callout label="Olympiad trap" warn>
  Average velocity is $\Delta x / \Delta t$, not the average of initial and
  final speeds. That shortcut only works when acceleration is constant.
  Round-trip average speed problems punish this relentlessly.
</Callout>

## Solve it here

<Problem
  id="np-k1d-1"
  number={1}
  title="The late train"
  difficulty="Easy"
  answer={375}
  unit="m"
>
  A train moving at 30 m/s applies its brakes and decelerates uniformly at
  1.2 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping, in meters?

  <Hint>
    No time is given, so use the time-free equation
    $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x$.
  </Hint>
  <Solution>
    Set the final velocity to zero and solve for the displacement:

    $$\Delta x = \frac{v_0^2}{2a} = \frac{30^2}{2(1.2)} = 375 \text{ m}$$
  </Solution>
</Problem>

<Problem
  id="np-k1d-2"
  number={2}
  title="Two-speed road trip"
  difficulty="Normal"
  answer={48}
  unit="mph"
>
  You drive the first half of a trip's *distance* at 40 mph and the second
  half at 60 mph. What is your average speed for the whole trip, in mph?

  <Hint>
    Equal distances take unequal times. Compute the total time for a distance
    $2d$ and divide.
  </Hint>
  <Solution>
    For each half of length $d$, the times are $d/40$ and $d/60$. So

    $$\bar v = \frac{2d}{\frac{d}{40}+\frac{d}{60}} = \frac{2}{\frac{1}{40}+\frac{1}{60}} = 48 \text{ mph}$$

    This is the harmonic mean, always less than the naive average of 50 mph.
  </Solution>
</Problem>
