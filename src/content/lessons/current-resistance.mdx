## Current is a flow rate

Current measures how much charge passes a cross-section per second:

$$I = \frac{dQ}{dt}$$

Inside a wire the carriers are not fast. They drift at $v_d$, and if there are
$n$ carriers per volume each with charge $q$, the current through area $A$ is

$$I = nqv_dA$$

Drift speeds in copper are under a millimetre per second. The signal travels at
nearly the speed of light because the field establishes itself throughout the
wire almost instantly, not because electrons race down it.

## Resistance is geometry times material

Resistance is not a property of a substance. Resistivity $\rho$ is. Resistance
is what you get after you commit to a shape:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{A}$$

Double the length, double the resistance. Double the cross-section, halve it.
This is why a problem that stretches a wire to twice its length at constant
volume gives four times the resistance: $L$ doubles and $A$ halves.

Ohm's law then relates the three quantities:

$$V = IR$$

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  Ohm's law is a description of certain materials, not a law of nature. Diodes,
  filaments, gas discharges and electrolytes all violate it. A tungsten bulb
  heats up as it conducts, its resistivity climbs, and its current-voltage
  curve bends over. When a problem gives you a nonlinear element, work with the
  actual curve and use $R = V/I$ only as a local ratio at one operating point.
</Aside>

## Reduction and power

Series elements carry the same current, so their voltages add and their
resistances add. Parallel elements share the same voltage, so their currents
add and their conductances add. Collapse a network by finding any pair that is
unambiguously in one configuration or the other, replace it, and repeat.

Power delivered to any element is the product of the current through it and the
voltage across it:

$$P = IV = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R}$$

The three forms are equal only for an ohmic resistor. Pick the one whose
variable is held fixed. In series the current is common, so $I^2R$ says the
largest resistor dissipates the most. In parallel the voltage is common, so
$V^2/R$ says the smallest resistor dissipates the most. Getting that backwards
is the most common error in a mixed network.
