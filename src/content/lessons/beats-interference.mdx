## Beats

Add two waves of nearly equal frequency at one point and use the sum-to-product
identity:

$$\cos(\omega_1 t) + \cos(\omega_2 t) = 2\cos\!\left(\frac{\Delta\omega}{2}t\right)\cos(\bar{\omega}t)$$

The second factor is a fast oscillation at the average frequency. The first is
a slow envelope. Loudness peaks whenever that envelope reaches an extreme, and
it does so twice per envelope cycle, so the beat rate is $|f_1 - f_2|$, not
half of it.

Tune a string against a 440 Hz fork and hear 3 beats per second and the string
is at 437 or 443. Tighten it: if the beats slow down you were flat.

## Two sources in space

Now hold the frequency fixed and move in space. Two sources driven in phase
send waves to a point along paths differing by $\Delta r$. Each extra
wavelength of path costs a full cycle of phase:

$$\Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\Delta r$$

Constructive interference needs $\Delta\phi$ to be a multiple of $2\pi$, so
$\Delta r = m\lambda$. Destructive needs the crest of one to meet the trough of
the other: $\Delta r = (m + \tfrac{1}{2})\lambda$.

That is the entire rule, and it carries over unchanged to light. Beats are the
same physics in time; two-source interference is the same physics in space.

## Adding the source phase

If the sources themselves are out of step by $\phi_0$, the total phase
difference is $\phi_0 + 2\pi\Delta r/\lambda$, and the conditions swap when
$\phi_0 = \pi$. Two speakers wired with reversed polarity are silent on the
perpendicular bisector, where the path difference is zero — the point that
would otherwise be the loudest.

Write down the total phase difference first, from all causes. Then ask what
makes it a multiple of $2\pi$. Never start from a memorized $m\lambda$.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  Perfectly destructive interference requires equal amplitudes as well as
  opposite phase. Two sources at different distances deliver different
  amplitudes, so a real minimum is quiet, not silent. Problems that ask for
  intensity, not just position, punish this.
</Aside>
