## Pressure with depth

Pressure is force per unit area, and in a fluid at rest it acts equally in every
direction — a fact you can feel by turning your head underwater and noticing
nothing changes.

Take a column of fluid of height $h$ and cross-section $A$. Its weight,
$\rho g h A$, must be supported by the pressure difference between top and
bottom, so

$$P = P_0 + \rho g h$$

Depth is all that matters. Two points at the same level in the same connected
fluid are at the same pressure, whatever the shape of the container — the water
in a narrow straw and in a wide tank beside it stand level. The shape of the
vessel never enters the formula, which is why a thin tube of water can burst a
barrel.

At the surface of a lake, $P_0$ is atmospheric, about $10^5$ Pa. Ten metres of
water adds another $10^5$ Pa, so the pressure doubles.

## Archimedes

Now replace part of the fluid with an object. The surrounding fluid pushes
harder on the bottom than the top, and that imbalance is the buoyant force.

$$F_B = \rho_{\text{fluid}} \, V_{\text{disp}} \, g$$

It equals the weight of the fluid displaced — no more, no less. The argument is
worth holding onto: imagine the object replaced by fluid of the same shape. That
fluid sat there in equilibrium, so the push from its surroundings exactly
matched its weight. The surroundings do not know what is inside, so the push is
unchanged.

Note the density in the formula is the *fluid's*. The object's density decides
only what happens next.

## Float, sink, or hover

Compare the buoyant force on a fully submerged object with its weight. Since
both scale with the same volume, only densities matter: denser than the fluid
and it sinks, less dense and it rises until part of it is out.

For a floating body, equilibrium requires $F_B = mg$, so
$\rho_f V_{\text{sub}} g = \rho_o V g$, giving

$$\frac{V_{\text{sub}}}{V} = \frac{\rho_{\text{object}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}}$$

Ice at $917$ kg/m³ in seawater at $1025$ floats with $89\%$ of its volume under
— the origin of the iceberg cliché, and a one-line calculation.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  $V_{\text{disp}}$ is the submerged volume, not the whole volume, and not the
  volume of the object's material. A hollow steel hull floats because it
  displaces a hull-shaped region of water; melt it into a solid block and the
  displaced volume collapses, so it sinks.
</Aside>
