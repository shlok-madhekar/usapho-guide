## The short list

Everything ordinary is built from twelve fermions in two families.

**Quarks** carry colour charge and feel the strong force. Six of them, in three
pairs of increasing mass: up and down, charm and strange, top and bottom. Their
electric charges are fractional — $+\tfrac{2}{3}$ for up-type,
$-\tfrac{1}{3}$ for down-type. They are never found alone, only in bound
groups: a **baryon** is three quarks, a **meson** is a quark and an antiquark.
A proton is *uud*, giving charge $\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{2}{3} - \tfrac{1}{3} = 1$;
a neutron is *udd*, giving zero.

**Leptons** carry no colour and ignore the strong force. Electron, muon and tau,
each paired with its neutrino. The muon is an electron 207 times heavier and
otherwise identical, which is why cosmic-ray muons behave like heavy electrons.

Every one of these has an antiparticle with opposite charge and identical mass.

## Four interactions

Each force acts through an exchanged boson. The **strong** force is carried by
gluons, acts on colour charge, and reaches about $10^{-15}$ m — roughly one
nucleus. **Electromagnetism** is carried by the photon, acts on electric
charge, and has infinite range. The **weak** force is carried by the W and Z
bosons, acts on every fermion including neutrinos, and reaches only about
$10^{-18}$ m. **Gravity** acts on mass-energy at infinite range and is
irrelevant at this scale, roughly $10^{-38}$ times the strong force between two
protons.

Massless carriers give infinite range; the W and Z are heavy, which is why the
weak force is short-ranged and slow. Beta decay is a weak process, and that is
exactly why free neutrons take about fifteen minutes rather than nanoseconds to
decay.

## What is allowed

A reaction happens only if every conserved quantity balances. Energy, momentum
and charge you already know. Three more matter here.

**Baryon number** $B$: each quark counts $+\tfrac{1}{3}$, so a proton or neutron
is $+1$ and an antiproton $-1$. Mesons are zero. $B$ is conserved in every known
process, which is why the proton, as the lightest baryon, has nothing to decay
into.

**Lepton number** $L$: $+1$ for each lepton, $-1$ for each antilepton, and it is
conserved separately for the electron, muon and tau families. This is what
forces an antineutrino into $n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$ — the electron brings
$L_e = +1$, so something with $L_e = -1$ must accompany it.

**Strangeness** is conserved by the strong and electromagnetic forces but
violated by the weak force. That asymmetry is why strange particles are made
quickly in pairs and then decay slowly.

<Aside label="Where this goes wrong" warn>
  Check every conservation law before declaring a reaction possible, and check
  the mass budget separately. A process can satisfy all the quantum numbers and
  still be forbidden because the products are heavier than the available energy.
</Aside>
